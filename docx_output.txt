Fundamental Operations (Addition) Place-Value Decomposition (Partitioning) Concept / Trick Explanation Break numbers into hundreds, tens, and ones - then calculate each part separately. This works because of place value: 45 = 40 + 5. Smaller chunks reduce mental overload. Step-by-Step Method First, split each number into tens and ones. Add or subtract the tens together. Add or subtract the ones together. Finally, combine the results. Worked Example 1 45 + 87 45 = 40 + 5 87 = 80 + 7 40 + 80 = 120 5 + 7 = 12 120 + 12 = 132 Final Answer: 132 Worked Example 2 85 - 22 85 = 80 + 5 22 = 20 + 2 80 - 20 = 60 5 - 2 = 3 60 + 3 = 63 Final Answer: 63 Worked Example 3 368 + 254 368 = 300 + 60 + 8 254 = 200 + 50 + 4 300 + 200 = 500 60 + 50 = 110 8 + 4 = 12 500 + 110 = 610 610 + 12 = 622 Final Answer: 622 Sequential Chunking (Incremental) Concept / Trick Explanation Keep one number whole, then add/subtract the other number in chunks (usually tens, then ones). This works because splitting 32 into 30 + 2 keeps each step easy and reduces mental load. Step-by-Step Method First, keep the starting number as-is. Now split the other number into tens and ones. Add/subtract the tens chunk first. Then add/subtract the ones chunk. Finally, say the result confidently - you built it step-by-step. Worked Example 1 56 + 32 56 + 30 = 86 86 + 2 = 88 Final Answer: 88 Worked Example 2 53 - 28 53 - 20 = 33 33 - 8 = 25 Final Answer: 25 Worked Example 3 247 + 68 247 + 60 = 307 307 + 8 = 315 Final Answer: 315 Compensation (Rounding &amp; Adjusting) Concept / Trick Explanation Change a tricky number into a nearby round number (like 30 or 40), then adjust at the end. This works because round numbers are faster to calculate, and small adjustments are easy to fix mentally. Step-by-Step Method First, identify the awkward number (like 29 or 39). Round it up or down to the nearest 10 (or 100). Do the easier calculation with the round number. Now adjust by adding or subtracting the difference. State the corrected final answer. Worked Example 1 45 + 29 29 is close to 30 45 + 30 = 75 But we added 1 too much 75 - 1 = 74 Final Answer: 74 Worked Example 2 75 - 39 39 is close to 40 75 - 40 = 35 But we subtracted 1 too much 35 + 1 = 36 Final Answer: 36 Worked Example 3 248 + 99 99 is close to 100 248 + 100 = 348 But we added 1 too much 348 - 1 = 347 Final Answer: 347 Fundamental Operation (Subtraction) Place Value Decomposition (Partitioning) Concept / Trick Explanation Break numbers into hundreds, tens, and ones, then calculate each part separately. Works because place value lets you handle smaller chunks instead of one large number. Step-by-Step Method Split both numbers into place-value parts. Add or subtract each place separately. Combine the partial results. Say the total clearly. Works because you process one simple step at a time. Step-by-Step Method Keep the first number unchanged. Split the second number into tens and ones. Handle the tens first. Then handle the ones. Worked Example 1 56 + 30 = 86 86 + 2 = 88 Final Answer: 88 Worked Example 2 53 - 20 = 33 33 - 8 = 25 Final Answer: 25 Worked Example 3 247 + 60 = 307 307 + 8 = 315 Final Answer: 315 Compensation (Rounding &amp; Adjusting) Concept / Trick Explanation Round a tricky number to a clean 10 or 100, then fix the difference. Works because round numbers are faster to compute. Step-by-Step Method Identify the awkward number. Round it to the nearest 10 or 100. Do the easier calculation. Adjust for the extra or missing amount. Worked Example 1 45 + 30 = 75 75 - 1 = 74 Final Answer: 74 Worked Example 2 75 - 40 = 35 35 + 1 = 36 Final Answer: 36 Worked Example 3 248 + 100 = 348 348 - 1 = 347 Final Answer: 347 Add-Up Subtraction (Complementary Addition) Concept / Trick Explanation Instead of subtracting, count up from the smaller number to the larger number. Works because adding up in friendly jumps is often easier than subtracting. Step-by-Step Method Start at the smaller number. Jump to the next friendly number (like a multiple of 10). Continue jumping until you reach the larger number. Add all jumps together. Works because the difference between numbers stays the same. Step-by-Step Method Identify a small adjustment that makes numbers cleaner. Add or subtract that amount from both numbers. Solve the easier subtraction.
